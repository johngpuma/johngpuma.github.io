The Puma: a Catan 1v1 Bot Trained from Scratch by Self-Play Reinforcement learning

A Catan 1v1 bot that learned entirely by playing against itself, with no human games and no imitation, trained on a home gaming PC. A 279K-parameter graph neural network reads the board as a graph of corners and edges and predicts both the best move and who's winning. At play time, an AlphaZero-style tree search guided by that network runs about 1,000 simulations per move.

779M Training positions
4.0M Self-play games
197/200 vs. Catanatron AlphaBeta
60% vs. a 1462-rated ladder bot
  • Training: PPO over 2,885 iterations and about 4.0 million self-play games, or 779 million positions, in 46 hours of compute on one Ryzen 5 3600 and a GTX 1080. Average game length fell from 174 turns to 87 as it improved.
  • Systems: 11 CPU worker processes play games in parallel and send positions through shared memory to a single GPU process, which evaluates them in batches of about 130. That pipeline sustained 4,625 positions/s and 15,700 network evaluations/s.
  • Reward: a small shaping reward helped early games finish and faded to zero by iteration 200. After that, the only reward was winning.
  • Against engines: using the network alone, with no search, it won 195–200 of 200 games against Catanatron's AlphaBeta bot at every one of 29 checkpoints from 389M to 769M positions.
  • Against ranked bots: in 200-game matches on the OpenHex ladder (100 boards, seats swapped, refereed by the opponent's own engine, about ±7 points), it scored 60% against a neural-network bot rated 1462 with one second of search a move, and an even 52% against a search bot rated about 1700.
  • Against people: 11–3 so far against human players at its practice table, a Colonist-style browser arena I built and open-sourced. It plays fair: it sees only what a player at the table could know, never your hand or your development cards.
