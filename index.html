Featured project

The Puma

A Catan bot that taught itself to play.

I trained a Catan 1v1 bot from scratch on my gaming PC. It never saw a human game and never copied another bot: it learned entirely by playing against itself, about four million games in two days of compute.

779Mtraining positions
4.0Mgames against itself
46 hof compute on one gaming PC
79%win rate against people so far (11–3)
  • How it thinks: a 279K-parameter graph neural network reads the board as a graph of corners and edges and predicts both the best move and who's winning. At play time, an AlphaZero-style tree search guided by that network runs about 1,000 simulations per move.
  • How it learned: PPO over 2,885 iterations. A small shaping reward helped early games finish and faded to zero by iteration 200; after that, the only reward was winning. Average game length fell from 174 turns to 87 as it improved.
  • The pipeline: 11 CPU workers play games in parallel and send positions through shared memory to one GPU process, which evaluates them in batches of about 130. It sustained 4,625 positions per second on a Ryzen 5 3600 and a GTX 1080.
  • It plays fair: it sees only what a player at the table could know, never your hand or your development cards. You can play it best-of-five in a Colonist-style browser arena I built and open-sourced.
